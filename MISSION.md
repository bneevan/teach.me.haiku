## Why
Develop the instinctive creative ability to capture brief, resonant human and natural moments in precise 3-line verse, moving fluently between rapid spontaneous snapshots and layered, contemplative poetry that evokes profound emotional resonance in the reader.

## Success looks like
- Write spontaneous "instant" haiku in under 60 seconds using the 3-beat sensory capture technique without artificial filler words.
- Compose deep, contemplative haiku utilizing [*toriawase*](./GLOSSARY.md#toriawase) (calculated two-image juxtaposition) and [*kireji*](./GLOSSARY.md#kireji) (the cutting pause).
- Accurately count phonetic units ([*on*](./GLOSSARY.md#on) / syllables) and understand why 17 English syllables often feels bloated compared to 17 Japanese *on*.
- Identify and select effective [*kigo*](./GLOSSARY.md#kigo) (seasonal anchors) to create cultural and ecological depth without explicit explanation.
- Distinguish true haiku from [senryu](./GLOSSARY.md#senryu) (human foibles/humor) and pseudo-haiku (abstract prose chopped into 5-7-5).
- Capture empirical reality through objective sensory sketching ([*shasei*](./GLOSSARY.md#shasei)), compose single-object poems ([*ichibutsu-jitate*](./GLOSSARY.md#ichibutsu-jitate)), and evoke the weathered transience of [*wabi-sabi*](./GLOSSARY.md#wabi-sabi) and [*mono no aware*](./GLOSSARY.md#mono-no-aware).

## Constraints
- Modern English haiku conventions (focusing on brevity, sensory immediacy, and 10–14 syllable cadence) prioritized alongside classical Japanese roots (5-7-5 onji).
- Eyes-free audio accessibility paired with ears-free visual reference.

## Out of scope
- Full renga / renku linked-verse multi-author composition parties.
- Classical Edo-period Japanese archaic grammar rules beyond foundational structural terms ([*kireji*](./GLOSSARY.md#kireji), [*kigo*](./GLOSSARY.md#kigo), [*on*](./GLOSSARY.md#on)).
