Curated High-Trust Bibliography

Haiku Knowledge & Wisdom Sources

Curated texts for craft knowledge and active communities for practitioner wisdom. Plain markdown version: RESOURCES.md.

Knowledge (Foundational Texts & Standards)

The Haiku Handbook: How to Write, Share, and Teach Haiku

By William J. Higginson & Penny Harter

The definitive 20th-century manual on the history, aesthetics, and cross-cultural craft in both Japanese and English.

Use for: Mechanics of English syllables vs. Japanese on, historical lineage from Bashō through Shiki to modern free-form.

Traces of Dreams: Landscape, Cultural Memory, and the Poetry of Bashō

By Haruo Shirane (Stanford University Press)

Masterful scholarly analysis of Bashō’s poetic vision, exploring how classical seasonal words (kigo) function as anchors of communal memory and emotional geography.

Use for: Understanding fueki (the unchanging) vs. ryūkō (the contemporary fluid) and seasonal depth.

Haiku: An Anthology of Japanese Poems and Modern English Haiku (4 Volumes)

By R.H. Blyth

The legendary four-volume compilation and Zen aesthetic commentary on the great Edo masters: Bashō, Buson, Issa, and Shiki.

Use for: Authentic historical translations and seasonal nature studies.

Haiku Society of America (HSA) Official Standards & Archives

Haiku Society of America

Official definitions, historical archives, and contemporary prize-winning anthologies defining modern English haiku conventions.

Use for: Understanding why modern English haiku ranges from 10–14 syllables rather than rigid 5-7-5.

Frogpond: The Journal of the Haiku Society of America

Published triannually

The premier international print journal showcasing current peer-reviewed haiku, senryu, haibun, and critical essays.

Use for: Studying cutting-edge models and contemporary craft debates.

Wisdom (Active Communities & Critiques)

The Haiku Foundation (THF)

Global Non-Profit Community

Global hub hosting Haikupedia, weekly writing prompts, digital library archives, and audio recordings of master poets reading their work.

Use for: Weekly composition challenges, audio listening, and community feedback.

r/haiku (Reddit Community)

Peer Community Forum

Active online forum for testing new haiku drafts, discussing syllable cadences, and receiving constructive peer critiques.

Use for: Quick feedback on line breaks and cutting words.
