Canonical Terminology

Haiku Poetics Glossary

The precise language, mechanics, and aesthetics established in this workspace. Also available in plain markdown: GLOSSARY.md.

Structural Mechanics

On (音 / Onji) [phonetic unit]
A Japanese phonetic time unit (mora) that measures acoustic duration. A single English syllable typically contains 1.5 to 2 Japanese on.
Avoid: Syllables, beats, word count
Kireji (切れ字) [structural cut]
A structural "cutting word" (or in English, a dash, colon, ellipsis, or line break) that bisects the poem into two distinct syntactic and perceptual planes.
Avoid: Punctuation mark, pause button
Kigo (季語) [seasonal anchor]
A designated seasonal word or phrase that anchors the poem in an ecological calendar and invokes shared cultural memory.
Avoid: Weather word, nature tag
Saijiki (歳時記) [seasonal almanac]
An organized compendium or almanac of recognized kigo, indexed by the four traditional seasons plus the New Year.
Avoid: Haiku dictionary, rhyming dictionary
Teikei (定型) [traditional form]
The traditional fixed formal pattern of 5-7-5 phonetic units (on).
Avoid: Haiku meter, rigid rule

Compositional Strategies

Toriawase (取り合わせ) [juxtaposition]
The juxtaposition of two disparate, independent sensory images that create a cognitive spark or third unwritten meaning across the cut.
Avoid: Contrast, collage, comparison
Ichibutsu-jitate (一物仕立て) [single-focus]
A "one-thing composition" focusing entirely on a single subject or sensory event without an overt juxtaposition.
Avoid: Single-topic poem, flat description
Yohaku (余白) [white space]
The pregnant "empty white space" or unspoken resonance left behind for the reader's imagination to complete.
Avoid: Blank space, vagueness, ambiguity
Shasei (写生) [sketch from life]
Sketching directly from life; objective observation of empirical sensory phenomena without emotional editorializing or poetic embellishment.
Avoid: Realistic writing, nature journaling

Aesthetic Sensibilities

Mono no aware (物の哀れ) [pathos of things]
A poignant, gentle awareness of the transience and impermanence of all worldly things.
Avoid: Sadness, depression, nostalgia
Wabi-sabi (侘寂) [rustic austerity]
An aesthetic celebrating rustic simplicity, quiet austerity, imperfection, and the natural patina of time.
Avoid: Vintage vibe, rustic chic
Karumi (軽み) [lightness]
A quality of lightness, casualness, and unforced ease championed by Bashō in his late years.
Avoid: Triviality, silliness, fluff
Senryu (川柳) [human irony]
A 3-line poetic form identical in structure to haiku, but centered on human foibles, irony, humor, and psychological absurdity rather than nature or season.
Avoid: Funny haiku, satirical poem
